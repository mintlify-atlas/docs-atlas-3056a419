As you apply to multiple roles, you'll quickly accumulate many tailored versions of your resume. Without a system, this turns into a folder of files named things like `resume_final_v3_ACTUAL_FINAL.docx` — and you'll have no idea which one you sent to which company when the interview call comes in two weeks later. This skill helps you build and maintain a master resume that serves as your single source of truth, an organized library of tailored copies with a consistent naming convention, and an application tracker that connects each version to a specific submission so nothing gets lost or confused.

## Version Structure

The master-plus-tailored model keeps your resume system clean regardless of how many roles you're pursuing simultaneously.

**Your master resume** is a comprehensive, unabridged document containing every experience, project, achievement, skill, and certification from your career. It's intentionally longer than a submission-ready resume — it's the full library, not a curated selection. You never send the master resume directly to an employer. Its purpose is to be the canonical source from which all tailored versions are derived. Every time you add a project, earn a certification, or quantify a new achievement, you update the master first.

**Tailored versions** are company- and role-specific derivatives of the master, trimmed and adjusted to match a specific job posting. Each tailored version is a copy made from the master — never a copy of another tailored version, which would introduce drift and make it impossible to track what's different from your baseline. A tailored version captures exactly what you presented to a specific employer at a specific moment in time.

## Naming Convention

A consistent naming convention makes it trivial to identify any version at a glance, find the file you need during an interview prep session, and audit your application history at the end of a job search.

**Recommended format:**

```text
[LastName]-Resume-[Company]-[Role]-[YYYY-MM].pdf

Examples:
Smith-Resume-Stripe-PM-2024-01.pdf
Smith-Resume-Google-DataScientist-2024-02.pdf
Smith-Resume-Acme-SeniorEngineer-2024-02.pdf
Smith-Resume-General-Master.docx
```

Use the full company name, not an abbreviation you might forget. Use a role label that matches the job title you applied for, abbreviated to fit. Include the year and month of submission — not creation date, but the date you actually applied. Keep the master file in `.docx` format so it's easy to edit; keep tailored submission files as PDFs so formatting is locked.

For role-type base versions (a well-tailored template for a category of role rather than a specific company), use:

```text
Smith-Resume-PM-Base-2024.docx
Smith-Resume-Engineering-Base-2024.docx
Smith-Resume-DataScience-Base-2024.docx
```

## What the Skill Tracks

The version manager maintains a complete record of your application activity, making it easy to follow up, analyze patterns, and prepare for interviews:

- **Which version was sent to which company** — never guess again what you submitted
- **Date submitted** — know exactly when to follow up and how long each company takes to respond
- **Tailoring changes made** — a summary of what was adjusted from the master for each version, useful for interview prep
- **Application method** — portal submission, employee referral, direct email, LinkedIn Easy Apply
- **Response received** — interview advance, rejection, or no response (ghosted), logged as you hear back
- **Interview stage reached** — phone screen, technical, hiring manager, panel, offer, or declined
- **Performance analysis** — which versions, role types, or companies have the best response rates over time, surfacing what's working

## Application Tracker

A simple spreadsheet tracks every submission and connects it to its version. Maintain this alongside your file folder:


## Master Resume Workflow

The master-to-tailored workflow is a one-way flow. Always start at the master — never build a tailored version from another tailored version.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Maintain the Master">
    Your master resume contains everything: all experience entries with 5–8 bullets per role (far more than you'd use in a submission), multiple summary variants written for different role types, a complete skills list organized by category, all certifications and education, every project worth mentioning, and quantified achievements even if the numbers are rough estimates you'll refine later. Update the master whenever you complete a significant project, earn a credential, get a promotion, or want to capture a fresh achievement before you forget the details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy, Don't Edit">
    When you need a tailored version, duplicate the master file. Never open the master and save over it. Create a copy with a descriptive name, then work on the copy. This guarantees the master is always untouched and you can always start fresh from a clean slate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tailor the Copy">
    Run the `resume-tailor` skill on the copy with the specific job description. Select the most relevant bullets from the master's full list, choose the appropriate summary variant, reorder the skills section, and trim the document to the right length. Document what you changed in the application tracker's "Notes" column.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Export and Name Correctly">
    Export the final tailored version as a PDF (locked formatting) using the naming convention above. Keep the `.docx` source file as well in case you need to make a last-minute correction before a deadline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log the Submission">
    Before you close your laptop, add a row to the application tracker: company, role, filename, application date, method. This takes 60 seconds and prevents the "wait, did I already apply there?" confusion that happens two weeks into an active job search.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log the Response">
    When you hear back — or when enough time has passed to confirm you haven't — update the tracker with the response type and stage reached. Over time, this data tells you which role types, industries, companies, and resume approaches are generating the best response rates. It also tells you which applications need a follow-up email.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Version Management Best Practices

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Do" icon="check">
    - Always start from the master as your source of truth
    - Use a consistent naming convention from day one
    - Track every submission in the application tracker the same day you apply
    - Keep the master updated when you complete new work
    - Date your files at the month level, not by version number
    - Back up to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud) automatically
    - Archive old versions instead of deleting them
  </Card>
  <Card title="Don't" icon="xmark">
    - Edit the master resume directly for any specific application
    - Use vague file names like `resume_v2_final_REAL.docx`
    - Build a tailored version from another tailored version
    - Let more than 90 days pass without reviewing whether the master is current
    - Maintain multiple files that could all be "the master"
    - Delete old versions — you may need to reference a past submission
    - Submit the same version to every company without any adjustment
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## When to Update the Master

The master resume should reflect your current reality at all times. Update it immediately when:

- You start or leave a job
- You complete a major project with quantifiable outcomes
- You earn a certification or complete a relevant course
- You receive an award, promotion, or significant recognition
- You develop proficiency in a new tool, language, or skill

Schedule a **quarterly review** to add recent achievements, refresh metrics with updated numbers (e.g., a product you launched that now has user data), remove experience that's becoming stale, and audit the skills section for relevance. Set a calendar reminder and treat it like a maintenance task — 30 minutes, four times a year, keeps your master perpetually ready.

<Tip>
  Start your master resume immediately — even if you're not actively job searching. It's far easier to document achievements when they're fresh than to reconstruct them six months later from memory and old performance reviews. Your master resume is also the foundation for your LinkedIn profile, annual performance self-evaluations, and any consulting or freelance proposals you might write. The investment pays off in every professional context.
</Tip>

## Performance Analysis

After running an active job search for 4–6 weeks, the application tracker contains enough data to draw useful conclusions. Look for patterns in:

**What's working:** Which role types, company sizes, or industries have the highest interview advance rate? Which versions of your summary or which bullet emphasis styles generate more responses?

**What needs adjustment:** If applications to a specific role type consistently go unanswered, the version you're using for those roles may need a full review — or the match score may be too low across the board.

**Where to focus effort:** If referral applications convert at 3× the rate of portal applications, that's a signal to invest more time in networking and less in cold applications. The tracker makes this visible.
