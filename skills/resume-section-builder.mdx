A resume section that works brilliantly for a senior executive will actively hurt an entry-level candidate — and vice versa. The Professional Summary that anchors a VP's resume should be dropped entirely for a recent graduate who doesn't yet have the track record to fill it with credible claims. The Education section that leads a new grad's resume gets buried at the bottom once you've accumulated 5 years of experience. Every section has a different priority, different content strategy, and different signals it needs to send depending on who you are and what you're applying for. The `resume-section-builder` skill accounts for all of this — it asks about your experience level and target roles first, then builds each section with the right structure, right content, and right placement for your specific situation.

## Experience Level Strategies

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Entry Level">
    **Profile:** 0–3 years of experience, recent graduates, candidates making a first career move

    **Core strategy:** You're selling potential, not an established track record. Lead with evidence of capability — your education, relevant projects, and any internship or part-time work where you produced real results. Use your Skills section prominently to signal that you have the tools the role requires even if you don't yet have years of experience wielding them.

    **Section priorities:**
    - Lead with **Education** — degree, GPA (if 3.5+), honors, and relevant coursework
    - **Projects** section is essential — academic projects, personal builds, capstone work, hackathon entries
    - **Skills** section placed high, before Experience
    - **Internships and part-time roles** go in Work Experience and get full bullet treatment
    - **Activities and leadership** (clubs, student organizations, volunteer work) can substitute for sparse professional experience
    - Skip the Professional Summary — you don't yet have enough to fill it credibly without resorting to clichés

    **Bullet focus:** Initiative, learning speed, accuracy, volume, and any measurable contribution even at small scale. "Completed onboarding training in 2 weeks ahead of the 4-week schedule" and "Handled 30+ customer inquiries daily with zero escalations during 3-month internship" are both strong entry-level bullets.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Mid-Level">
    **Profile:** 4–10 years of experience, established professionals, candidates demonstrating progression

    **Core strategy:** You're selling a demonstrated track record with clear upward trajectory. Lead with your Work Experience — your recent roles are your strongest asset. Use a Professional Summary if you have a specific narrative to tell (career transition, specialized expertise, or targeting a stretch role). Show that your scope and responsibility have grown over time.

    **Section priorities:**
    - Lead with **Work Experience** — this is your primary evidence
    - **Professional Summary** (3–4 sentences) if you have a clear value proposition to anchor the reader
    - **Skills** section after Experience — signal technical depth without leading with it
    - **Education** moves to the bottom — degree and year, GPA only if exceptional (3.8+)
    - **Certifications** included if role-relevant (PMP, AWS, CPA, etc.)
    - Drop coursework and academic achievements from Education

    **Bullet focus:** Ownership, measurable outcomes, scope growth across roles. Show that your most recent role has larger impact and broader responsibilities than your first. Promotions and title changes should be clearly legible in the formatting.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Senior">
    **Profile:** 10+ years of experience, senior individual contributors, managers, directors, and above

    **Core strategy:** You're selling strategic impact and organizational leadership. Lead with an Executive Summary that immediately frames the scale of your experience (P&L ownership, team size, organizational scope). Every bullet should demonstrate influence at the organizational level — decisions you made, strategies you set, transformations you drove. The reader should understand within 10 seconds that you operate at a senior level.

    **Section priorities:**
    - Lead with an **Executive Summary** — 3–5 sentences covering your leadership scope, domain expertise, and signature accomplishments
    - **Career Highlights** block (optional) — 3–4 one-line standout achievements between the summary and full experience history
    - **Work Experience** in full detail for last 10–15 years; older roles compressed to name/title/dates only
    - **Board Roles / Advisory** included as a separate section if applicable
    - **Education** at the bottom — degree and school only; year is optional (protects against age bias)
    - **Certifications and continued education** signals ongoing development

    **Bullet focus:** Strategic scope, financial impact, organizational change. Team size, budget owned, P&L responsibility, and market impact. "Led the company's first international expansion into 4 European markets, generating $12M ARR in year one" is the register you're writing at.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Section-Specific Guidance

### Professional Summary

The summary is 3–5 sentences at the top of your resume that frame everything that follows. It works by giving recruiters a mental model of who you are before they start scanning — when a summary is strong, readers interpret your bullets through a favorable lens.

**Formula:** `[Title or identity] + [Years / scope of experience] + [2–3 signature capabilities] + [What you bring to the target role]`

```text
Entry-level example:
Recent Computer Science graduate from UC Berkeley with internship experience
in full-stack development. Skilled in Python, React, and AWS. Seeking to apply
academic machine learning projects and production-grade internship work to a
software engineering role in a product-led environment.

Mid-career example:
Product Manager with 7 years driving B2B SaaS products from 0 to scale.
Track record of shipping products that generated $10M+ ARR through rigorous
prioritization and cross-functional alignment. Deep expertise in API products,
developer tooling, and enterprise sales motions.

Senior example:
Technology executive with 16 years building and scaling engineering
organizations from 50 to 500+ across global markets. P&L ownership of $100M+
with a track record of 40%+ efficiency improvements through platform
modernization and Agile transformation. Recognized for building high-retention
cultures in high-growth environments.
```

**Never include in a summary:** "seeking a challenging opportunity," "hard-working team player," "results-oriented professional," or anything written in the third person. These phrases signal that you haven't thought carefully about what you actually offer.

### Work Experience

The core of your resume. Each role entry should include company name, city/state, your title, and dates. Bullets should follow the CAR or X-Y-Z framework — achievement-focused, metric-anchored, role-relevant.

**Bullet count guidelines:**
- Most recent role: 4–6 bullets
- Role 2–3: 3–5 bullets
- Roles 4+: 2–3 bullets
- Roles 10+ years old: title, company, dates only (unless particularly significant)

**Handling promotions at the same company:**

```text
COMPANY NAME | City, State
Senior Manager, Product        2022 – Present
Manager, Product               2020 – 2022
Associate Product Manager      2018 – 2020

• Most impactful bullet from current level
• Second bullet showing strategic scope
```

Listing promotions separately makes career progression visible at a glance — one of the strongest signals you can give a hiring manager.

### Skills

The Skills section's job is to confirm that you have the technical toolkit the role requires and to give the ATS the exact keyword strings it's searching for.

**For technical roles:** Organize by category — Languages, Frameworks, Cloud/Infrastructure, Tools. Include version numbers only when they're material (Python 3.x is fine; Python 3.11.2 is noise).

**For business roles:** Group as Technical Tools (Salesforce, Tableau, Excel), Methodologies (Agile, Lean, Six Sigma), and Domain Knowledge (financial modeling, enterprise sales, supply chain).

**What to leave out:** Microsoft Word and PowerPoint (assumed for almost every role), any skill you'd stumble on if asked about it in an interview, vague soft skills listed as line items ("team player," "strong communicator") — these belong in your bullets as demonstrated capabilities, not in a list.

### Education

After your first 3–5 years of professional experience, Education should be brief and factual. The decision points are:

- **GPA:** include if 3.5 or above and you graduated within the last 5 years; otherwise omit
- **Honors:** include Magna/Summa Cum Laude; omit Dean's List unless entry-level
- **Coursework:** only for entry-level candidates using it to demonstrate relevant knowledge
- **Year:** always include for mid-career; optional for senior candidates (protects against age bias)

### Projects

The Projects section is most valuable for three groups: recent graduates without extensive work history, career changers demonstrating new skills outside their day job, and technical candidates with open-source contributions or personal builds that show applied skill.

```text
PROJECTS
E-commerce Marketplace | React, Node.js, PostgreSQL | github.com/user/project
• Built full-stack platform with 500+ active users and Stripe payment integration
• Implemented full test suite with 90% code coverage; deployed via Docker on AWS

Machine Learning Churn Predictor | Python, scikit-learn, Pandas
• Trained classification model achieving 87% accuracy on 50K-row customer dataset
• Reduced false-positive rate by 30% vs. baseline logistic regression model
```

<Note>
  The skill will ask about your experience level and target roles before generating sections. This isn't just gathering context — it's required. The same person applying to an IC engineering role and a Director of Engineering role needs two structurally different resumes, and the section builder produces the right structure for each target rather than a one-size-fits-all template.
</Note>

## Section Quality Checklist

Before finalizing your resume sections, verify:

- ✅ Section order is optimized for your experience level and target role
- ✅ Summary is present (if mid-career or senior) and free of clichés
- ✅ Skills are relevant, organized, and include the keywords from your target job postings
- ✅ Experience bullets lead with achievements, not duties
- ✅ Education section matches your career stage (right level of detail)
- ✅ Additional sections (Projects, Certifications, Volunteer) add value — not filler
- ✅ Every section uses consistent formatting
- ✅ Nothing irrelevant or outdated is included
- ✅ Total resume length matches experience level (1 page or 2 pages)
- ✅ All sections support the specific role you're targeting
