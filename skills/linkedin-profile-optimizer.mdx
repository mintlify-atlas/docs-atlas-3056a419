Recruiters search LinkedIn daily for candidates — and the algorithm surfaces profiles based on keyword density, completeness, and activity signals, not just connections. A poorly optimized profile means qualified candidates get filtered out before a single human reads their name. This skill optimizes every section of your LinkedIn profile for both the algorithm and human readers: syncing your experience with your resume, rewriting your headline and About section for recruiter search queries, and injecting the specific keywords that surface you in the right searches at the right time.

## What Gets Optimized

The skill works through every major LinkedIn section in sequence, applying targeted improvements to each:

- **Headline** — the single most-searched field on LinkedIn; optimized for keyword density and clarity
- **About section** — the first three lines visible before "see more" are rewritten to hook both the algorithm and the reader; the full section is restructured as a narrative with embedded keywords
- **Experience bullets** — mirrored from your resume achievements but expanded with more context and industry vocabulary
- **Skills section** — gaps are identified against your target role's keyword requirements; up to 50 skills are populated to maximize endorsement surface area
- **Education** — standardized formatting; relevant coursework or honors added when applicable
- **Featured section** — recommendations for what to pin (projects, posts, press, portfolio links) to maximize profile completeness signals

## LinkedIn vs. Resume: Key Differences

LinkedIn and your resume serve different audiences and different moments in the hiring process. Treating them as identical documents leaves value on the table.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Tone">
    Resumes use a formal, third-person-implied voice — no pronouns, compressed phrasing. LinkedIn rewards first-person, conversational writing. Your About section should read like a confident, well-written professional speaking to another professional, not a formatted document. "I help SaaS companies turn product ideas into revenue" outperforms "Experienced Product Manager with 8 years in SaaS" as an opener on LinkedIn.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Keywords">
    Resume keywords are optimized for a single job description — you tailor them per application. LinkedIn keywords must cover your entire target role category, because recruiters search across all candidate profiles simultaneously. Cast a wider net: include job title variations, tool synonyms, and adjacent terminology that might appear in any posting for your target role.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Length">
    Resumes are constrained to one or two pages. LinkedIn has no hard limits. Use the space: longer About sections, more bullets per role, media attachments, and a populated Featured section all increase your completeness score and recruiter visibility. The algorithm rewards thoroughness.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Completeness">
    LinkedIn explicitly filters incomplete profiles from recruiter search results. A resume missing a section is unusual; a LinkedIn profile missing the About section, Skills, or current role description is actively penalized. Completeness is a ranking signal, not a cosmetic concern.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Headline Formula

Your headline is the most important field on LinkedIn for recruiter search. It appears in search result listings, connection requests, post bylines, and comment threads — which means it's your most-repeated first impression. At 220 characters, it needs to do three things: identify your role, signal your value, and pack in searchable keywords.

<Tip>
  The first 220 characters of your headline are shown in search results and on mobile. Pack in 3–4 keyword-rich terms that recruiters search for your role — job title variations, your primary domain, and your most differentiating skill or achievement signal.
</Tip>

Three proven headline patterns, with examples for each:

**Pattern 1: `[Title] | [Value Prop] | [Key Skill]`**
```text
Senior Product Manager | B2B SaaS | Driving 0→1 Products from Concept to $10M ARR
Data Scientist | Machine Learning & Analytics | Turning Complex Data into Business Decisions
```

**Pattern 2: `[Title] helping [audience] achieve [outcome]`**
```text
Software Engineer helping fintech teams ship reliable payments infrastructure at scale
UX Designer helping consumer apps reduce friction and improve activation by 40%+
```

**Pattern 3: `[Title] @ [Current Company] | [Unique Differentiator]`**
```text
Staff Engineer @ Stripe | Distributed Systems | Previously Google, Databricks
Marketing Director @ Series B Startup | 3x Revenue Growth | PLG & Brand Strategy
```

**Headlines to avoid:**
- `Looking for opportunities` — signals unemployment without signaling value
- `Student at University of X` — use your field of study and target role instead
- `Open to work` — use LinkedIn's official Open to Work feature; don't waste headline characters on it
- Just your current job title — wastes 200 characters of searchable real estate

## LinkedIn Profile Optimization Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Profile Audit">
    The skill assesses your current profile completeness (LinkedIn's All-Star threshold and beyond), identifies missing sections, flags keyword gaps against your target role, and notes any inconsistencies between your LinkedIn and resume that could confuse recruiters comparing the two.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keyword Research">
    Using your target role and industry, the skill extracts a keyword set from common job descriptions for that role — critical skills (tools, technologies, certifications), important soft skills (leadership, communication patterns), and industry terminology that signals familiarity with the domain. These are then mapped against your current profile for gap analysis.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Headline Rewrite">
    The skill drafts three headline variants using the formulas above, each emphasizing a different angle — role + achievement, role + audience + outcome, role + company + differentiator. You choose the one that feels most authentic, or the skill combines elements from multiple drafts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="About Section Rewrite">
    The skill rewrites your About section as a three-to-five paragraph narrative: an attention-grabbing first line (visible before "see more"), a paragraph on who you are and what you do, a paragraph on your standout achievements and specialties, a paragraph on what you're looking for or excited about, and a closing call to action with contact information. Keywords are woven in naturally throughout.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Experience Section Enhancement">
    Each role is reviewed for completeness and impact. The skill expands thin descriptions, adds achievement-focused bullets mirroring your resume, and suggests media attachments (links, presentations, or portfolio pieces) that would strengthen the role entry.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Skills and Completeness Sweep">
    The skill identifies missing skills from the keyword research, recommends which 50 to populate, and provides a final completeness checklist — photo, banner, About section, current role, two past roles, education, five-plus skills, and 50-plus connections — against LinkedIn's All-Star threshold requirements.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## About Section Structure

The About section is your only opportunity for long-form narrative on LinkedIn. Use it to tell your professional story, embed keywords, and give recruiters a reason to reach out. Here is the recommended structure:

```text
[HOOK — First line visible before "see more". Make it a statement, not a summary.]

[WHO YOU ARE — 2–3 sentences on your role, domain, and how long you've been doing it.]

[WHAT YOU'VE ACHIEVED — Your 2–3 biggest career highlights, with metrics where possible.]

[WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING FOR or WHAT EXCITES YOU — Signal your direction and values.]

[SKILLS LIST — A dense, comma-separated block of 10–15 keywords that recruiters search.]

[CALL TO ACTION — How to reach you. Email, LinkedIn message, or portfolio link.]
```

**Example first lines by role:**
- Product Manager: `"I help SaaS teams turn ambiguous customer problems into products people actually want to use."`
- Software Engineer: `"I build the infrastructure that makes fast companies faster — currently focused on distributed systems and developer tooling."`
- Marketing Leader: `"Brand is what you say. Growth is what you prove. I do both."`

## SSI Score Factors

LinkedIn's Social Selling Index (SSI) is a 0–100 score that measures how active and complete your professional presence is. Recruiters at companies with LinkedIn Talent Solutions can filter candidate searches by SSI threshold — meaning a low score can render you invisible to sourcing.

SSI is composed of four equally weighted pillars:

| Pillar | What It Measures | How to Improve |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Professional brand | Profile completeness, thought leadership content | Complete all sections, publish posts or articles |
| Finding the right people | Searching and viewing profiles in your field | Search for peers, target companies, industry leaders |
| Engaging with insights | Liking, commenting, and sharing industry content | Engage with 3–5 posts daily |
| Building relationships | Connection requests sent and accepted | Connect with people you've worked with and meet |

A complete, keyword-rich profile maintained by the skill's recommendations will move your SSI score meaningfully — particularly the professional brand pillar, which is directly tied to profile completeness and headline optimization.

## Recruiter Visibility Settings

Beyond content, several LinkedIn settings affect whether recruiters can find and contact you:

- **Open to Work** — enable in Career Interests settings with your target roles and locations; choose "Recruiters only" for discretion if currently employed
- **Profile visibility** — set to "Public" to appear in Google searches and recruiter tools outside LinkedIn
- **Sharing profile edits** — consider turning off while making bulk changes to avoid flooding your network's feed with update notifications
- **Contact and personal info** — ensure your professional email is visible to connections; add your phone number if you're actively searching

## Best Practices

- **Update LinkedIn before starting a search, not during.** Sudden profile completeness improvements combined with viewing many company pages is a readable signal that you've recently started looking. Get your profile to its optimized state in one session.
- **Keep your LinkedIn and resume dates consistent.** Discrepancies between your resume and LinkedIn employment dates are the most common red flag in background screening. Review both side by side after optimization.
- **Use LinkedIn's native features, not workarounds.** The algorithm boosts engagement with native video, newsletters, and articles more than links to external content. If you're building a personal brand alongside your job search, post natively.
- **Recommendations are the most human signal on the platform.** Ask for two to three targeted recommendations from former managers or cross-functional partners; give them talking points so the recommendations speak directly to the skills relevant to your target role.
- **Keywords in recommendations count.** If a recommender mentions your expertise in "machine learning pipelines" or "enterprise sales cycles," that text is indexed and searchable. Brief your recommenders on the skills you want highlighted.
