Most resume bullets read like job descriptions — they describe what a role involved rather than what the person actually accomplished. "Responsible for customer support" tells a recruiter nothing about scale, quality, or impact. The `resume-bullet-writer` skill rewrites every bullet using proven frameworks that lead with action, anchor on measurable outcomes, and signal the scope of your contribution. The result is a resume that tells a story of consistent achievement rather than a list of duties you were assigned.

## The CAR Framework

The skill's primary writing framework is **CAR: Challenge → Action → Result**. It mirrors how hiring managers think when they evaluate candidates — they want to know what problem existed, what you specifically did about it, and what demonstrably changed as a result.

- **Challenge** — What situation, problem, or goal set the context? (A failing metric, an untapped opportunity, a scaling bottleneck, a new initiative)
- **Action** — What did *you* do? Use first-person active verbs. Be specific about your direct contribution, especially on team efforts.
- **Result** — What measurably changed? Use numbers, percentages, dollar amounts, time savings, or before/after comparisons.

**Why CAR works:** It forces every bullet to contain all three components that differentiate high-performers from job-holders. Recruiters scan for this pattern consciously — a bullet that shows result without action feels unearned; action without result feels incomplete.

The skill also supports the **X-Y-Z formula** (Google's preferred format): *"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"* — which maps directly to CAR with the result stated first for maximum impact at the start of the line.

## How the Rewrite Process Works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the Weak Pattern">
    The skill reads each bullet and classifies the failure mode: passive language (`responsible for`, `assisted with`, `helped`), missing metrics (no numbers anywhere), vague scope (`large team`, `various clients`, `multiple projects`), or duty-framing (describes a task rather than an outcome). Each bullet gets a diagnosis before any rewriting begins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask Targeted Discovery Questions">
    Before writing a single word, the skill asks you the questions needed to gather real data. For a bullet like "Managed social media," it will ask: What platforms? What were the follower counts at start and end? What specific tactics did you use? What business impact resulted (leads, traffic, conversions)? This phase ensures the rewritten bullet reflects your actual work — not a generic template.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply the Framework">
    With your answers in hand, the skill applies CAR or X-Y-Z to structure the bullet. It selects a strong action verb from category-appropriate lists (leadership, growth, optimization, problem-solving), integrates the quantified result prominently, and keeps the bullet to 1–2 lines maximum.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calibrate Tone and Length">
    The skill adjusts tone for your target role and seniority level. An entry-level bullet emphasizes initiative and learning velocity. A senior bullet leads with strategic scope and organizational impact. Every rewritten bullet is trimmed to remove filler words while preserving all the information that makes it credible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Present Before/After with Explanation">
    Each bullet is delivered as a before/after pair with a brief explanation of exactly what changed and why. This helps you understand the principles so you can apply them yourself going forward.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When Metrics Are Unknown

Not everyone has tracked exact numbers throughout their career — that's normal. When you don't have a precise figure, the skill uses estimation strategies to arrive at defensible, conservative numbers:

- **Approximate from memory** — if you think resolution time dropped by about half, the skill will suggest "~50%" and ask you to confirm
- **Calculate from inputs** — if you met with 5 clients per week for a year, the total is "250+ client consultations annually"
- **Use conservative bounds** — estimates are always rounded down: if you think it was 60%, the bullet says "~50%"
- **Range notation** — "Managed team of 8–12" or "Generated $50K–$75K monthly" when headcount or revenue fluctuated
- **Relative comparisons** — if absolute numbers are unavailable, relative markers still work: "ranked in top 10% of sales team," "first in department to achieve..."

All estimated figures are clearly marked in the draft with `[estimated]` or `~` notation, prompting you to verify before final submission.

<Warning>
  Never fabricate metrics. The skill generates estimates when real numbers aren't available — but estimates need to be grounded in your actual memory of the work. Interviewers will ask about specific numbers on your resume. If you can't explain how you arrived at a figure, remove it or replace it with a range you can confidently defend.
</Warning>

## Bullet Quality Checklist

Before every bullet goes on your resume, it should pass all of these checks:

- ✅ Opens with a strong action verb (no "responsible for," "helped," "assisted")
- ✅ Contains at least one concrete number or metric
- ✅ Shows a result or outcome — not just an activity
- ✅ Gives context of scale (team size, budget, user count, account value)
- ✅ Stays within 1–2 lines
- ✅ Reads as an achievement, not a duty
- ✅ Uses language relevant to the target role
- ✅ Can be explained and defended in an interview

## Special Situations

**Entry-level / recent graduates:** The skill will mine academic projects, internships, club leadership, and volunteer work for quantifiable achievements. Example: "Led university marketing club of 50 members, organizing 8 events attended by 300+ students and securing $10K in corporate sponsorships."

**Career changers:** The skill reframes bullets from your old industry using vocabulary that maps to the target field. The underlying achievement stays the same; the framing shifts to match the hiring context.

**Employment gaps:** Freelance projects, volunteer roles, and professional development are written with the same achievement framework — there's no reason a gap period has to look empty on paper.
